June 19 marks Juneteenth, a day commemorating the emancipation of enslaved people in the United States. 

On June 19, 1865, more than two years after the Emancipation Proclamation was issued, the last enslaved African Americans in Galveston, Texas were informed of their freedom.

Juneteenth stands as a powerful reminder of resilience, progress, and the continued pursuit of equality. Today, it is observed across the country as a time to reflect on history, celebrate culture, and recognize the contributions of Black Americans to our communities and our nation.
