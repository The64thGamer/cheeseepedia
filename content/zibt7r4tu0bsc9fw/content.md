***Maple Heights, CA (5376 Northfield Rd)*** was a [Pizza Time Theatre] that opened on October 28th, 1980, and closed in January 1986

### History

***

##### Pre-Opening

Before Pizza Time Theatre moved in, the building was originally a Kurtz Furniture store that shut down in the late 70s after the company went out of business after 74 years in operation [1].

APCOA, Inc., an airport parking company based in Cleveland, Ohio is the franchisee and headed by Chairman Edwin M. Roth, with his son Corey Roth who is managing the restaurant operations ventures [2]

By October 1980, "coming soon" signage was shown outside the building, and the franchise began running employment advertisements to recruit its inaugural staff [3].

##### 1980s

On October 28th, 1980, the Pizza Time Theatre at 5376 Northfield Rd officially opened at Southgate USA Shopping Center, right next to the Southgate Cinema 5 [4]. As the 18th Pizza Time Theatre to ever open, the 11,000-square-foot restaurant was remodeled by A. Siegler & Associates of the former furniture store, and also include the former Mawby restaurant, would cost more than $200,000 to remodel [5]. The location opened with Madame Oink as their Guest Star and Dolli Dimples as the Cabaret act, arcade games, a salad bar, an additional private dining area, and theatre room with the Portrait Stage, controlled with the
Cyberamics Control Center [6]

APCOA, Inc. would later operate locations in Ohio, Texas, and Georgia. In late 1981, the franchisee was taken over by the Electronic Theatre Restaurants Corporation, who will become the largest franchisee for Pizza Time Theatre, operating 16 stores by 1982 [7][8].

Maple Heights would replace Madame Oink with Harmony Howlette sometime in 1981. By 1983, the restaurant would likely have received Helen Henny as the permanent guest-star.

In January 1986, the franchisee closed Maple Heights Pizza Time Theatre for unknown reasons, but possibly was underperforming. The assets were auctioned off on January 26th, 1986 at 10 A.M, by Rosan & Company, Inc [9][10].

##### Post-Closure

In the late 1980s, the former building would become a Sherman-Williams Floor World paint store [11]. The building will remodel their exterior through the 1990s with dividing the space into other business, with the Southgate Cinema 5, which was right next to the former Pizza Time Theatre, would close in 1998, and would eventually be demolished in May of 2005 [12]. Currently, the former Pizza Time Theatre space is vacant, with the U.S. Bank closing in early 2020 in a small part of the former space.
