The ***Country Bear Jamboree*** is an elaborate Audio-Animatronic show produced by Walt Disney Imagineering that first appeared upon the opening of Walt Disney World on October 1, 1971, and was later added to Disneyland and Tokyo Disneyland. The show consists of 5 stages, 22 characters (18 of whom are bears), and 25 A-100 figures in one theater, Grizzly Hall.

## Background

Before Walt Disney World even began construction, plans for a musical animatronic show featuring bears were already being considered by Disney. Walt's idea for a show like this first came about in around 1965, when the company was planning to create a ski resort in the Mineral King Valley area of what is now Sequoia National Park in California. For the project, Walt assigned fellow Imagineer Marc Davis to design the bear characters. Davis, with the help of Imagineer Al Bertino, came up with several types of bear shows.

Even after Walt passed away on December 15, 1966, the bear show idea would still be worked on. However, the plans for the ski resort came to an indefinite halt when the development of the Mineral King Valley was prevented due to environmental concerns. Along with the ski resort, the bear show idea was shelved.

When planning began for Walt Disney World, the bear show concept came back, and Imagineers decided that the bears would be put there.

## History

### Early Years

The Country Bear Jamboree debuted with the rest of Magic Kingdom and Walt Disney World on October 1, 1971. Located in Frontierland, the show was state of the art for the time, and upon opening, it was a hit with park guests, becoming an E-Ticket attraction due to its immense popularity. The attraction's high attendance outweighed the attendance of the other three animatronic shows at the park at the time, those being the Mickey Mouse Revue, Hall of Presidents, and Tropical Serenade (later renamed Walt Disney's Enchanted Tiki Room).

After seeing the massive success of the Country Bears at Disney World, the Disney company decided to add a version of the show to Disneyland. The addition of the Country Bears to Disneyland made the show the first attraction in Disney park history to be cloned from Walt Disney World instead of Disneyland. For Disneyland's show, two theaters, each with a full set of Audio-Animatronics, were built out of anticipation for the show to be popular there as well. The Country Bear Jamboree opened at Disneyland on March 24, 1972, in the new Bear Country (later Critter Country and now Bayou Country) area of the park. However, in stark contrast to its Disney World counterpart, the Disneyland show was nowhere near as popular, the theaters often being near empty.

The Country Bears performed solely at Walt Disney World and Disneyland for 11 years until April 15, 1983, when a third and final version opened at Tokyo Disneyland alongside the rest of the park, located in the Westernland area.

### Closure of the Disneyland Show

On September 9, 2001, the Country Bear Jamboree at Disneyland permanently closed. Following the attraction's closure, the show building was expanded and completely overhauled to become The Many Adventures of Winnie the Pooh, a dark ride, which still remains at the park today. Most of the characters were sent to Walt Disney World to be used as parts.

On The Many Adventures of Winnie the Pooh, Imagineers included a tribute to the Country Bear Jamboree by placing static versions of Buff, Max, and Melvin above the backside of the entrance to one of the rooms. These static versions of the three characters came from the Hungry Bear Restaurant (now Pooh Corner); they had functioned while they were at the restaurant. Additionally, in one of the scenes, Pooh descends from a hot air balloon, the lowering mechanism for which had been previously used for Teddi Barra's swing.

The internal mechanism of one of the Big Al animatronics was partially repurposed for the Oogey-Boogey animatronic in the Haunted Mansion Holiday overlay of the Haunted Mansion ride.

### Refurbishment at Walt Disney World

On August 21, 2012, the Country Bear Jamboree closed at Walt Disney World for an almost two-month-long refurbishment. During this time, the characters' looks, especially their fur and facial details, would be changed slightly, their mechanics would be fully restored, and the show would be edited down from 15 minutes and 55 seconds to roughly 12 minutes. This edited version of the original show cut much of the in-between and extra dialogue, removed "Fractured Folk Song" and "Pretty Little Devilish Mary," and moved "If You Can't Bite, Don't Growl" to where "Fractured Folk Song" was. The show reopened on October 17, 2012. In the years that would come, the guest traffic of the show would see a considerable but not drastic decline, with some years being steeper than others.

### Announcement of the New Version at Walt Disney World

At the Destination D23 event on September 9, 2023, it was announced that the Country Bear Jamboree at Walt Disney World would be receiving a complete overhaul and new show. This meant that the original show would be replaced entirely with a brand new script and setlist featuring mostly Disney songs, as well as new voiceover work. The new show was to be called the Country Bear Musical Jamboree.

### Final Performance of the Original Show at Walt Disney World

On January 27, 2024, the final show of the original Country Bear Jamboree was held. This final performance, in addition to being attended, crowded in, and heavily filmed by devoted Disney fans, became rather odd; when Henry's curtains opened at the beginning of the show, he did not move at all while his audio played. One attendee, who was among the many guests who filmed the final show, called Henry's frozen state "T-posing Henry," as a result of his unflattering position. Henry stayed like this throughout the entirety of "Bear Band Serenade." Funnily enough, when the curtains opened on Liver Lips McGrowl for "My Woman Ain't Pretty (But She Don't Swear None)," he too was completely still in his idle position. Following the end of the performance, it was revealed that there was an issue with the turntable that both the first Henry bot and Liver Lips shared. In both awkward instances of the characters being lifeless, the attendees made the best of it. Thankfully, though, all the other characters worked normally, including the other two Henry bots, though some were more worn out than others. When time came for "Blood on the Saddle," sung by Big Al, the audience erupted with intense, loud excitement, even chanting Big Al's name until his curtains opened. Everyone sang the song louder than they did they other songs.

Until the malfunctions occurred, there were supposed to be three more performances that evening. After the show ended, the guests waited in the queue for four hours, waiting to see if the cast members could get the show running again. One cast member held a very long speech about the importance of the Country Bears, the cast members' dedication to the show, and the legacy that the original show would leave, calling the long-waiting guests "troopers" for waiting so long. Unfortunately, the show could not run again that night, making the malfunctioned performance the very last performance of the original show at Walt Disney World. To end the night on a good note, the cast members allowed the guests to walk through the theater one last time before its six-month-long closure, take final pictures of the theater's original form, and even sing some of the original songs with some of the cast members up front.

### Opening of the Country Bear Musical Jamboree

The Country Bear Musical Jamboree held its soft opening on July 11, 2024, but it officially opened on July 17. The new show featured the characters reoutfitted, refurred, and moving more fluidly than ever before. In addition to the cosmetic changes, there were technological changes too, with the characters now running on Compliance. Also, Liver Lips McGrowl was now Romeo McGrowl, and Henry's name officially became Henry Bear. To pay homage to the original show, "Come Again (Come On In)" remained as the end of the show, albeit with new instrumental composition and voice work.

### Present Day

The Country Bear Musical Jamboree remains at Walt Disney World. At Tokyo Disneyland, the full-length original show still runs in rotation with the Vacation Hoedown version, though both are largely in Japanese. A few of the figures and props from Disneyland's show are in the Walt Disney Archives.

## Other Versions

### Christmas Special

In November 1984, the Country Bear Jamboree became the first attraction in Disney park history to have a holiday version. This temporary version of the show, known as the Country Bear Christmas Special, implemented both traditional songs and a few original tunes here and there. The Country Bear Christmas Special ran seasonally at Disneyland until December 2000, at Walt Disney World until December 2005, and at Tokyo Disneyland until 2019.

### Vacation Hoedown

In February 1986, the Country Bear Vacation Hoedown was introduced at Disneyland. A summery, vacation-themed show, the setlist featured both song covers and original songs just like the Christmas Special. This version became the permanent standard version at Disneyland. In May 1986, this version was introduced at Walt Disney World and ran there until February 1992. The Vacation Hoedown was introduced at Tokyo Disneyland on July 15, 1994, and has been running seasonally since.

## Sponsors

From 1971 to 1981, the Country Bear Jamboree at Walt Disney World was sponsored by Pepsi-Cola and Frito-Lay. At Disneyland from 1975 to 1995, the show was sponsored by Wonder Bread. The show is sponsored by House Foods at Tokyo Disneyland.
