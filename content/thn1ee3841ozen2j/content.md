***Westminster, CO (7121 N. Sheridan Blvd)*** was a [Pizza Time Theatre] that opened on December 9th, 1980, and closed in February of 1984.

## History

##### Pre-Opening

The development of the Westminster location began in August of 1980, when newspapers in Colorado began reporting on a new family entertainment restaurant coming to the area (1). On September 24, 1980, The Denver Post published an article titled "Chuck E. Cheese to Entertain Diners at New Pizza Outlet," announcing plans for a new Pizza Time Theatre at West 72nd Avenue and Sheridan Boulevard. The location was supposed to open in late November and would become the first of several Pizza Time Theatre restaurants planned throughout Colorado. 

The Colorado franchise was initially reported to be operated by TAG Enterprises Inc., based in Golden, Colorado, and headed by Gregory Hilligoss of Apton, California. Also Charter Management, CO., headed by William R. McCormick, serving as the company's director of operations and secretary-treasurer (2). The company planned to expand throughout the state, with future locations proposed for Boulder, Lakewood, southeast Denver, Colorado Springs, Fort Collins, and Pueblo. 

By November 1980, employment advertisements were already appearing for the upcoming restaurant as preparations continued for its opening.

##### 1980s

On December 9th, 1980, the Pizza Time Theatre at 7121 Sheridan Boulevard officially opened at the Shoenberg Shopping Center, with Charles Adams being the store's manager. Westminster was the 24th CEC to ever open, and the building being an approximately 8,200-square-foot restaurant reportedly costing around $750,000 to construct (3). The location opened with Madame Oink as the Guest Star and Dolli Dimples as the Cabaret act. It also featured the Warblettes, more than 55 arcade games, a salad bar, pizza, sandwiches, and a full Cyberamics Control Center (4). 

Westminster replaced Madame Oink with Harmony Howlette in September of 1981 (5), and received The King sometime in 1982. 

In May 1982, Westminster participated in several local promotions alongside the Boulder location. For Chuck E. Cheese's fifth birthday celebration, the restaurant offered a free Commemorative Poster by LeRoy Neiman to the first 300 customers who purchased a large pizza. Around this period (6).

In 1983, Westminster and Aurora even participated in a cow-milking contest sponsored by the Dairy Association, with Chuck E. Cheese and Mr. Munch (7).

Westminster would become one of the locations affected by the financial troubles of Pizza Time Theatre. The restaurant closed in February 1984 due the company's financial problems. A newspaper article titled "Refunds Offered Customers of Chuck E. Cheese Outlet" reported that both Westminster and Boulder restaurants were among approximately 20 Pizza Time Theatre locations that closed following the resignation of company founder Nolan Bushnell. Also discussed customers seeking refunds for deposits placed on birthday parties at the closed restaurants. 

##### Post-Closure

Following its closure, the former Pizza Time Theatre later reopened as the Westminster Dinner Theatre during the 1990s. In 2007, the original building was reportedly demolished to make room for additional parking associated with the nearby Walmart development. Today, the former Pizza Time Theatre site is currently a Walmart Supercenter.
