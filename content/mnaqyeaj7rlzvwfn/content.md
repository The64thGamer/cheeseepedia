***Max Sportsters*** was a short-lived chain of family entertainment centers with stores located in Northern California. It brand lasted from around 1995 when several *Chuck E. Cheese's*, owned by the *Ulrike-Grandjean Corporation*,[4] shuttered and were rebranded to focus on a sports-themed aesthetic.[3] Because it was only around for a short time, there isn't much information left about these stores today.

# History

*Max Sportsters* started around 1995 when *Chuck E. Cheese* franchise owner, *Ulrike-Grandjean Corporation*, closed down all of their locations in Northern California. They then re-themed the restaurants entirely to sports.[3] Most of the *Chuck E. Cheese's* former elements were kept around, such as the Cabarets and Lounges. These were converted into "sound-proof" quiet rooms where adults could sit away and enjoy silence. Other staples of the *Chuck E. Cheese* stores remained, like the kitchen and arcade areas.

#### Stages

One part of this brand that has remained elusive is the existence of a stage. No one has ever recalled Max Sportsters having any stage or animatronics of some sort. Unfortunately, there is not enough information to prove or deny the existence of the animatronic remaining.

#### Closure

The chain didn't last long. By the late 1990s, most all of the chain's stores had shuttered due to underperformance. The District Manager, Allen Strege, cited high overhead costs and that business declined during the summer as reasons for the closures. The last documented existence of the brand happened on June 23, 1999 when the announcement of [Tracy's ](https://cheeseepedia.org/?v=cep-js&=vij4t1rmqgl90kbj)*[Max Sportsters](https://cheeseepedia.org/?v=cep-js&=vij4t1rmqgl90kbj)* was reported.[1]

#### Post-Closure

After the closure of all of their stores, they would receive new tenets. All except for the [Marinwood store](https://cheeseepedia.org/?v=cep-js&=0navst45l3qr4c3j), they would remain vacant for years after. Finally coming down in June of 2022.[2]
