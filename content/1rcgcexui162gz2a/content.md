Trust is one of the hardest things a brand or a person can earn — and one of the easiest to lose.

What makes this recognition from USA TODAY mean so much is that it isn't about a campaign or a moment. It's about a relationship between a brand and the families who have trusted us for nearly 50 years. That kind of relationship comes with accountability. Parents have high expectations of us — and they should. Their kids deserve nothing less.

Trust, when broken, isn't restored with words. It's restored with thousands of quiet, consistent acts — a team member who remembers a child's name, a birthday party that goes exactly right, a family that walks out the door smiling when they almost didn't walk in.

I know this firsthand. I worked in Chuck E. Cheese stores as a teenager, and I took every birthday party I hosted seriously — even when there was plenty of human drama swirling around me. When that kid walked through the door, none of that mattered. Their moment was not going to be anything less than perfect on my watch.

20 plus years since being a birthday hostess at the Kendall Miami Chuck E Cheese, I know trust isn't built in a boardroom. It's built by the people who decide, every single shift, that whatever else is going on around them doesn't get to touch the kids who are enjoying being “Where A Kid Can Be A Kid”.

This brand has faced real challenges. What carried it through was an unshakeable commitment to purpose. What I'm watching now is more than a comeback — it's a renaissance. A brand that knows exactly who it is, who it's for, and why it shows up. 

Congrats to my peers and to the thousands of leaders, cast members, techs for your commitment to one “easy” purpose: Every Guest Leaves Happy!!!
