The ***Mickey Mouse Revue*** was a massive Audio-Animatronic theater show produced by Walt Disney Imagineering that ran at Walt Disney World from October 1, 1971, to September 14, 1980, and at Tokyo Disneyland from April 15, 1983, to May 25, 2009. The show featured 73 classic Disney characters and 81 Audio-Animatronics.

## Background

In an interview done in 1962, when describing his new Audio-Animatronics technology, Walt Disney mentioned having plans for "all of the Disney characters [1]. After his death, these plans would ultimately manifest as the Mickey Mouse Revue, intended as a tribute to him [2]. Imagineers John Hench, Bill Justice, and Wathel Rogers would contribute to the development of the Mickey Mouse Revue.

Originally, there were supposed to be 79 characters featured in the show as Audio-Animatronics, but 6 were scrapped, these being the Big Bad Wolf, Ferdy and Morty Fieldmouse, Geppetto, Horace Horsecollar, Humphrey the Bear, and Pinocchio. However, the Big Bad Wolf and Pinocchio were implemented, albeit in different ways, both appearing at the end of the preshow and the Big Bad Wolf appearing as a shadow projection during the show [2].

## Walt Disney World

### Opening and Early Years

The Mickey Mouse Revue opened with the rest of Magic Kingdom on October 1, 1971, as one of 4 animatronic shows in the park. Located in Fantasyland, the show was initially popular and was listed as an E-Ticket attraction. Amusingly, due to a planning mistake, the theater at Walt Disney World could seat 500 park guests, but the preshow area was only able to hold 300 [1]. This popularity was short-lived, however, thanks to the instant massive popularity of the [Country Bear Jamboree], by which the guest traffic of the Revue was severely outweighed. In the span of just a few years, the attendance of the Mickey Mouse Revue dwindled, eventually to the point where it was downlisted to a D-Ticket attraction. This decline continued through the rest of the attraction's tenure at Walt Disney World.

### Final Year at WDW

On May 14, 1980, several malfunctions occurred during the show, notably Pluto's head and Timothy Mouse not moving, as well as Rabbit's chair not moving until 10 seconds into the performance [2].

The Mickey Mouse Revue's last performance at Walt Disney World was held on September 14, 1980. Following this, the show was removed and shipped to Japan for the eventual opening of Tokyo Disneyland.

The theater where the Mickey Mouse Revue was sat vacant for 7 years before the 3D film *Magic Journeys* debuted on December 15, 1987. The film would be shown in the theater until December 1, 1993. On July 8, 1994, the *Legend of the Lion King* stage show took its place and ran until February 23, 2002. Since October 8, 2003, Mickey's PhilharMagic has occupied the theater.

## Tokyo Disneyland

### Pre-TD

During the planning for Tokyo Disneyland's attractions, representatives of Oriental Land, Co. toured Disneyland and Walt Disney World in the 1970s to choose attractions that would be at the future park. The Mickey Mouse Revue was among the chosen attractions, and the choice was made to have the show shipped to Japan, as that was less expensive than reproducing the set pieces and Audio-Animatronics. The show became the first attraction in Disney Park history to be shipped to Tokyo Disneyland from one of the American parks rather than being replicated, and the second to be shipped to another park without replication overall, following the Carousel of Progress [2].

### Re-Opening

On April 15, 1983, the Mickey Mouse Revue opened with the rest of Tokyo Disneyland, and like at Walt Disney World, was located in Fantasyland. There, it enjoyed far more success than it did in Florida and operated for much longer, being at Tokyo Disneyland for 26 years compared to it having been at Walt Disney World for only almost 9 years.

### Permanent Closure

The Mickey Mouse Revue held its final show on May 25, 2009, and permanently closed afterwards. After sitting vacant for almost 2 years, Mickey's PhilharMagic debuted in its place on January 24, 2011, and has been operating since.
